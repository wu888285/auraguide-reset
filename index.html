The real problem

You don’t have a motivation problem.

You have a system problem. Motivation is unreliable — it shows up when things are exciting and disappears by Wednesday. Discipline is different: it’s a small set of systems that make the right action the easy one, even on your worst days.

If you’re honest, you already know what to do. You just don’t do it. You plan a productive morning and wake up to your phone instead. You start strong on Monday and quit by Wednesday.

That isn’t weakness — that’s relying on willpower, which always runs out. Over the next 7 days you’ll install three systems, one at a time. Each takes under 10 minutes.

The three systems

Get clear. Make it easy. Protect the streak.

01 · Choose One Thing — not ten

  • Pick the single habit that would change the most. One. Not a list.
  • Shrink it until it’s almost too easy: “read one page,” not “read 30 minutes.”
  • State exactly when and where: “After I brush my teeth, I will do 5 push-ups.”

Why it works: vague goals get skipped. Specific, tiny actions get done — and small actions compound.

02 · Remove the Friction

  • Make the good thing 20 seconds easier: gym clothes out, water on your desk.
  • Make the bad thing 20 seconds harder: phone in another room, notifications off.
  • Design your environment once so you don’t fight yourself all day.

Why it works: you don’t rise to your goals, you fall to your environment.

03 · Never Miss Twice

  • Missing one day is harmless. Missing two becomes a new habit — so don’t allow it.
  • Lower the bar on hard days: a two-minute version still counts.
  • Check it off visibly every day. The streak itself becomes the reward.

Why it works: consistency beats intensity. 30 boring days beat a perfect week you abandon.
